## The Pattern

The technology works. The licence is in progress or obtained.
The governance layer does not exist yet.

This is the most common operational gap in regulated tokenization platforms —
and the most expensive one to fix after go-live.

---

## What "No Governance" Actually Means

It is rarely a complete absence of structure. It is usually this:

- A RACI exists on paper. Ownership breaks down at function boundaries.
- Vendor contracts are signed. Escalation paths are undefined.
- Compliance is tracked. Nobody owns the regulatory reporting calendar.
- The platform is live. The incident classification process was never built.

The gap is not visible in the technology. It surfaces in the first vendor dispute,
the first regulatory question, and the first operational incident.

---

## Where It Breaks — By Layer

| Layer | What is missing | When it becomes a problem |
|---|---|---|
| L0 — Operating Model | Decision rights undefined. RACI not operationalized. | First cross-functional blocker |
| L1 — Regulatory & Legal | Compliance embedded in legal, not in delivery. | First NCA question post-licensing |
| L2 — Structure & Contracts | SLA framework exists. Escalation procedure does not. | First vendor underperformance |
| L6 — Go-Live & Operations | Hypercare plan missing. KPI/KRI not defined. | First 30 days post go-live |
| L7 — Post-Launch, Steady State & Continuity | No audit calendar. No vendor SLA review cadence. | First regulatory audit |

---

## What Infrastructure Providers Get Wrong

**Treating governance as a post-launch task.**
Governance introduced at L5 or L6 is not governance. It is damage control.
Every week of delay in defining decision rights is a week of compounding risk.

**Confusing documentation with governance.**
A governance framework document is not a governance structure.
Governance is operational when every escalation has a named owner,
every vendor has a defined SLA review cadence,
and every regulatory obligation has a hard deadline on a tracked calendar.

**Scaling before the operating model is stable.**
Adding issuers, jurisdictions, or asset classes to an ungoverned platform
multiplies the operational risk — it does not dilute it.

---

## What Structured Governance Gives You

- Decision rights that hold under regulatory review
- Vendor escalation paths that do not require senior intervention for every issue
- A regulatory reporting calendar with named owners and hard deadlines
- An operational model that can onboard the next issuer without rebuilding from scratch
